How Much Concrete Do I Need for a 10x10 Slab?

Planning a 10x10 concrete slab? The amount of concrete you need depends mainly on the slab's length, width, and thickness. Here's a simple calculation you can use to estimate the concrete required.

Quick Answer: 10x10 Slab at 4 Inches Thick

Concrete Volume 33.33 ft³
Cubic Yards 1.23 yd³
Cubic Meters 0.94 m³

These figures are based on a slab measuring 10 feet long, 10 feet wide, and 4 inches thick. A small additional amount may be ordered to account for waste, uneven ground, spillage, or variations in the actual slab depth.

How to Calculate Concrete for a 10x10 Slab

The basic formula for calculating concrete volume is:

Length × Width × Thickness = Concrete Volume

Because the length and width are measured in feet, the thickness needs to be converted from inches to feet before calculating.

Step 1: Convert the thickness

A 4-inch slab has a thickness of:

4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet

Step 2: Multiply the dimensions

Now multiply the length, width, and thickness:

10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.33 cubic feet

So a 10x10 slab that is 4 inches thick requires approximately 33.33 cubic feet of concrete.

Convert Cubic Feet to Cubic Yards

Ready-mix concrete is commonly ordered by the cubic yard in the United States. There are 27 cubic feet in one cubic yard.

33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards

Therefore, the calculated volume is approximately 1.23 cubic yards.

Important: The calculated amount is the theoretical volume. Contractors may account for a small amount of extra concrete depending on the project and site conditions.

What If the Slab Is a Different Thickness?

Thickness makes a significant difference in the amount of concrete required. Here are approximate volumes for a 10x10 foot slab:

  • 3 inches thick: about 25 cubic feet
  • 4 inches thick: about 33.33 cubic feet
  • 5 inches thick: about 41.67 cubic feet
  • 6 inches thick: about 50 cubic feet

Always use the actual planned slab thickness when ordering concrete.

Should I Add Extra Concrete?

A calculated volume is an estimate based on the dimensions you enter. Real construction conditions can vary because of uneven surfaces, formwork, measurement differences, spillage, and other factors.

For that reason, many projects allow for a small additional quantity rather than ordering exactly the theoretical volume.
