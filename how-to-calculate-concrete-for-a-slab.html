How to Calculate Concrete for a Slab

Calculating the amount of concrete needed for a slab is simple when you know the slab's length, width, and thickness. This guide explains the formula and shows an easy example.

Concrete Slab Calculation Formula

The basic formula for calculating concrete volume is:

Length × Width × Thickness = Concrete Volume

The important thing is to use the same measurement unit for every dimension. If your slab thickness is given in inches while length and width are in feet, convert the thickness to feet first.

Step-by-Step Calculation

  1. Measure the slab length.
  2. Measure the slab width.
  3. Measure the required slab thickness.
  4. Convert the measurements to compatible units.
  5. Multiply length × width × thickness.
  6. Convert the result to cubic yards or cubic meters if needed.

Example: 10x10 Foot Slab

Let's calculate the concrete needed for a slab that is 10 feet long, 10 feet wide, and 4 inches thick.

First, convert 4 inches to feet:

4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet

Now calculate the volume:

10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.33 cubic feet

Convert cubic feet to cubic yards:

33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards

So the theoretical concrete volume is approximately 33.33 cubic feet, or 1.23 cubic yards.

How to Convert Concrete to Cubic Yards

If you calculate the volume in cubic feet, divide the result by 27 to convert it to cubic yards.

Cubic Feet ÷ 27 = Cubic Yards

For example, if your slab requires 54 cubic feet:

54 ÷ 27 = 2 cubic yards

How to Convert Concrete to Cubic Meters

To convert cubic feet to cubic meters, multiply the cubic-foot volume by approximately 0.0283168.

Cubic Feet × 0.0283168 = Cubic Meters

For example:

33.33 × 0.0283168 ≈ 0.94 cubic meters

Why Slab Thickness Matters

Thickness directly affects the amount of concrete required. A slab that is 6 inches thick will require more concrete than the same slab at 4 inches thick.

Before calculating your concrete quantity, make sure you know the planned thickness for your project.

Tip: Double-check your measurements before ordering concrete. Actual construction conditions can vary, so project planning may require an additional allowance.
