Concrete Slab Calculator for Garage

Building or replacing a garage floor requires an accurate estimate of the concrete volume needed. A concrete slab calculator can help you estimate the amount of concrete required using the garage floor length, width, and slab thickness.

This guide explains how to calculate concrete for a garage floor, convert the result into cubic feet and cubic yards, and account for an additional waste allowance.

How Much Concrete Do I Need for a Garage Floor?

The amount of concrete required for a garage slab depends mainly on the floor dimensions and the planned slab thickness.

  • Garage floor length
  • Garage floor width
  • Concrete slab thickness

Once these measurements are known, the concrete volume can be calculated before ordering ready-mix concrete or estimating bagged concrete.

Garage Concrete Formula

If the measurements are in feet, use this formula:

Concrete Volume = Length × Width × Thickness

Because slab thickness is often measured in inches, convert inches to feet before using the formula.

For example, a 4-inch slab is approximately 0.333 feet thick.

Example: Concrete for a 20 × 20 Garage

Suppose a garage floor is 20 feet long and 20 feet wide, with a 4-inch concrete slab.

Measurement Value
Length 20 feet
Width 20 feet
Thickness 4 inches

First convert the thickness to feet:

4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet

Now calculate the volume:

20 × 20 × 0.333 ≈ 133.2 cubic feet

Convert cubic feet to cubic yards:

133.2 ÷ 27 ≈ 4.93 cubic yards

So the basic estimate is approximately 4.93 cubic yards before adding a waste allowance.

What Thickness Should a Garage Slab Be?

Garage slab thickness depends on the type of garage, expected vehicle loads, soil conditions, reinforcement, local construction requirements, and other site-specific factors.

A 4-inch slab is commonly used in some residential applications, but it should not automatically be treated as the correct thickness for every garage project.

Important: For a garage project, consider local building requirements, site conditions, reinforcement, drainage, and expected loads before deciding on slab specifications.

Should You Add Waste to a Garage Concrete Estimate?

A concrete estimate may not exactly match the amount ultimately needed because real construction sites can have uneven areas, measurement differences, and other variations.

Adding a reasonable allowance can help create a more practical ordering estimate.

SlabCalc includes a waste percentage option so you can compare the base concrete volume with an estimate that includes additional material.

Concrete Bags for a Garage Floor

If you plan to use bagged concrete instead of ready-mix concrete, you need to know the yield of the particular product.

Concrete bags are available in different sizes, including common 40 lb, 60 lb, and 80 lb options. The amount of concrete produced by each bag varies by product.

Always check the manufacturer's stated yield on the bag before purchasing materials.

How Much Does Garage Concrete Cost?

The total cost of a garage concrete slab can include more than the concrete itself. Important cost factors may include:

  • Concrete material
  • Delivery
  • Site preparation
  • Excavation
  • Reinforcement
  • Labor
  • Finishing

Frequently Asked Questions

How much concrete does a 20 × 20 garage floor need?

A 20 × 20 garage floor with a 4-inch slab requires approximately 4.93 cubic yards of concrete before adding a waste allowance.

How do I calculate garage concrete in cubic yards?

Multiply the garage length by width and slab thickness in feet to get cubic feet. Then divide the result by 27 to convert cubic feet into cubic yards.

Is a 4-inch slab enough for a garage?

The required thickness depends on the garage design, expected loads, soil conditions, reinforcement, and applicable construction requirements. A 4-inch thickness should not be assumed to suit every garage.

Should I include waste when ordering garage concrete?

A reasonable allowance may help account for measurement differences and site variations. The appropriate amount depends on the specific project and site conditions.
